Slide 2 — Philosophy — The Ouroboros Approach (2:00)
Script:
  "My operating model is what I call the Ouroboros loop — it's a living system. We start by listening: to users, to designers, to partners. We align on the real problem. We build something light and useful, prove it with signals, scale what works, measure impact, and feed those learnings back so we can iterate. The loop keeps us moving without letting us harden around a single solution.
  Three pillars anchor my work: people-first clarity — make sure people can do their best work; trust, privacy and safety — because those are non-negotiable at product scale; and craft at velocity — we move fast but don't sacrifice quality. That rhythm allows craft and speed to coexist."
Visual prompt: Point to loop and then to three pillars.

Slide 3 — Why These Two Case Studies (1:30)
Script:
  "I chose two case studies because they show complementary strengths. The Meta engagement is a year-long, org-scale project: systems adoption, agency transformation, creator journeys — real scale across billions of users. Ring is the opposite pressure test: 90 days to create a working model and deliver against an AI sprint under a founder timeline. Both are about the same core work — building belief first, and structure second — but they show different constraints and different levers."
Transition: Frame the Meta case study as a deep-dive into scale.

CASE STUDY 1 — META (1 YEAR)

Slide 4 — Executive Summary (Meta) (2:00)
Script:
  "At Meta I worked across three orgs: The Agency, Blueprint DPA, and Creators & Publishers. The mission was to enable platform growth by bringing brand into product ops, raising design system adoption, and surfacing creator-first workflows. Key outcomes: Blueprint reached roughly 80% adoption; teams shipped ~20% faster; design debt went down about 35%. The Agency came together as a strategic partner and we delivered roughly 25 product priorities tied to growth metrics in Q1 '24. The throughline was consistent craft and trust baked into operations."
Emphasis: Read numbers with clarity — adoption, speed, design debt.

Slide 5 — People Problem + Business Need (Meta) (2:00)
Script:
  "The people problem was real: designers worked hard but felt disconnected from product impact. Systems were fragmented so quality varied across surfaces. From the business side, the ask was to increase creator retention, scale content volume, and improve monetization in a content-first pivot against intense competition. So the need was twofold: create a better experience for creators and users, and make the designer experience consistent and effective."
Transition: "Which led to three workstream-specific challenges…"

Slide 6 — Problem & Challenge (By Workstream) (2:30)
Script:
  "We broke the work into the three workstreams you see on-screen. The internal Agency had been measured on outputs — headcount and throughput — not outcomes. Blueprint DPA had only 15% adoption at baseline, leading teams to re-invent patterns and increase debt. Creator-facing work showed friction — especially in publishing flows, rights and originality, and surface consistency. The task was to connect these silos into an operational system that reduced friction for creators and designers while supporting growth goals."
Transition: Lead into stakeholders and how we coordinated.

Slide 7 — Stakeholders & XFN Partners (1:30)
Script:
  "This wasn't a design-only problem. I had to operate cross-functionally with Product Design, Content Design, UXR, Engineering, Brand, PM, Marketing, Legal/Policy for rights, and Data Science. Leadership included VPs and Directors across design and growth. Practically, that meant treating each group as a customer: listening to needs and designing rituals and artifacts that actually worked for them — from ICs to executives."
Visual cue: Point to network map and call out key bridges.

Slide 8 — Approach & Strategy (Systems + Rituals + Pods) (3:30)
Script:
  "We applied the Ouroboros in practice. First, we embedded pods: design, content design, UXR and engineering paired around product priorities so ownership and outcomes were aligned. Second, we built rituals — weekly cross-discipline crits, creative excellence checkpoints, and monthly growth syncs — to create visibility and feedback. Third, we invested in enablement: a Blueprint Learning Hub, an advocacy network, and a Design Systems Summit that drew 400+ attendees. Adoption was earned through lightweight learning and visible wins rather than top-down mandates."
Action: Briefly call out one example playbook or artifact (e.g., a Blueprint onboarding checklist) to make it concrete.

Slide 9 — Risks & Mitigation (1:30)
Script:
  "Every program has risks. For adoption risk we leaned into listening sessions and champions. For alignment risk we mapped work to a portfolio tracker and ran monthly growth reviews tied to OKRs. For quality risk — inconsistent craft — we introduced excellence checkpoints and piloted an AI quality guard to help flag accessibility and style regressions. And for trust/safety, we partnered closely with legal and policy to bake governance into workflows. The goal was to treat risk as design material — visible and actionable."
Transition: Move to measurable results.

Slide 10 — Outcomes & Impact (2:30)
Script:
  "The outcomes were measurable: Blueprint adoption climbed to 80%; shipping speed improved by about 20%; design debt decreased by ~35%; Agency was unified (45+ people) and produced 10 playbooks to scale practice; 25 product priorities were tracked to growth metrics. On the creator side, we reduced friction in publishing flows and improved retention signals for the platform that serves billions. These numbers mattered because they showed the system improved both designer experience and product outcomes."
Emphasize: Connect the metrics back to business goals and designer experience.

Slide 11 — What I'd Do Differently (1:00)
Script:
  "If I could iterate the program again, I'd stand up measurement in week one — get the signal sooner. I'd recruit the advocacy network earlier, before launch. And I'd make the Agency ↔ Blueprint ↔ Creators flywheel visible earlier so leaders could see compound effects faster. These changes would accelerate adoption and amplify early wins."
Transition: Close Meta story and pivot to Ring.

Slide 12 — Executive Summary (Ring) (2:00)

Slide 14 — Problem & Challenge (Ring) (1:30)

  "The challenge was twofold. First, there was no working model: no intake, no shared rituals, leadership transition complicating clarity. So the work was about designing an operating model that enabled rapid, aligned execution without creating bureaucracy."

Slide 16 — Approach & Strategy (Architecting the Model) (3:00)
Script:
  "We created concrete artifacts: a Unified Design Brief (UDB) to capture intent and constraints, a RASCI to clarify responsibilities, a Confluence wiki to surface playbooks and sprint kits, and leadership review decks for fast decision-making. Rituals included weekly cross-pod crits, leadership reviews, sprint planning, and lightning talks. We moved the org from service compartments to embedded pods — shared ownership and clearer handoffs. These artifacts and rituals made it possible to scale execution without heavy governance."

Sprint 3 aligned on strategy: prioritization, success metrics, validation and GTM.
